Earth Jukebox

NASA pictures of Earth, played as live music

What is going on

Six records, each one a NASA picture of Earth's oceans or atmosphere; some open a second page where you choose between the still map and a turning globe. The jukebox reads the colours as data and plays them live, while the picture stays on screen: a beam sweeps across the world and every kind of data has its own instrument. Choose a record, and the doors will open.

  • Pitch is latitude
  • Each kind of data has an instrument
  • Drums follow the region
  • Sad and urgent where the data is strong
Press a red button to choose a record.

Made for the NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026. An independent project, not an official NASA product. Pictures: NASA Scientific Visualization Studio.

Follow a current

Instead of sweeping every column, follow one current along its own flow lines. Pitch is the water temperature under the moving point (warmer is higher), the stereo position is where it is on the map, and the scale follows the region it passes through. Warm water turns the music sad, and very warm water urgent. The picture shows only the direction of the lines, not an arrow: the named currents use their known direction, and a start you choose heads east unless you tick Reverse.

Pick a named current, or enter a start point and press Follow.

El Niño layer

An add-on for this map. It lays NASA's monthly sea surface temperature anomaly (how much warmer or cooler than normal, 1982 to 2017) over the average temperature map, and adds NOAA's Niño 3.4 index, the standard measure of El Niño. When you press Play, the sweep also plays the anomaly under the beam: bright glass notes where the water is warmer than normal and dull kalimba notes where it is cooler, with higher pitch meaning warmer than normal. The music turns sad where it is warmer than normal, and urgent where it is much warmer. Grey boxes on the map mark places where NASA's own timeline covers the movie, so there is no anomaly data. Land and near-normal water are left clear, so the temperature map (shown in grey while the layer is on) shows through.

Tick "Add the El Niño layer", choose a month, and press Play, or press "Play the years" to hear the Niño 3.4 index from 1982 to the latest month while the maps change.

Click or tap the map to hear that spot. With a keyboard: arrow keys move (Shift = bigger steps), Home/End jump to the west/east edge, Enter replays the spot, Space plays or pauses.

Playback

Export the note trace

Every note played is recorded with its column, longitude or month, the data under it, the scale and mood, and the source file's fingerprint. Play something, then export.

With spoken narration on, pressing play first has a voice describe the image from west to east, then explain how to listen. Then the sweep plays once, with no talking over the music, and stops at the end. Clicking a spot also describes it aloud. If you use a screen reader, you may prefer to leave narration off and use the descriptions below.

How the map becomes music
Aerosols: NASA Scientific Visualization Studio, svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/5669. Ocean currents: NASA's ECCO2 ocean-model visualization. Sound generated live in your browser with the Web Audio API. A first version made for the NASA Space Apps "Earth Jukebox" challenge. Region labels are approximate.

Welcome to the Earth Jukebox

This turns NASA pictures of Earth's oceans and atmosphere into music. Pitch is the latitude, each kind of data has its own instrument, and the drums play faster where the data is stronger.

  1. Press Play to hear one sweep.
  2. Press Hear the instruments to learn what each voice means.
  3. Click or tap the picture to hear a single spot.
